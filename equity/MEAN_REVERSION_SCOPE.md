# What do we mean by mean reversion?

October 28, 2026. The user asked whether the weak short-horizon reversal result fits “random walk in the short run, mean reversion in the long run.” **It is compatible with weak short-horizon predictability, but it establishes neither a random walk nor long-run reversion.**

## Three distinct questions

1. **Short-run return predictability:** does yesterday's factor-relative return predict an opposite return from the next available entry? Our [1/5/20-session response experiment](experiments/equity_reversal_response/README.md) asks this. Every overall slope and gross-event-mean interval includes zero. This is failure to establish this signal, not proof that all available information is useless or that returns are independent.
2. **A stable relative price relationship:** does a specifically defined price spread pull back toward an equilibrium? Historical return correlation does not establish that property. Correlated stocks can drift permanently apart. A cointegrating price relationship, if proposed, needs its own definition, formation-only estimation, stability checks, and later validation. It is not implied by return PCA or H0 clustering.
3. **Long-horizon valuation and expected returns:** are future multi-year market returns associated with valuation measures? The Nobel committee's asset-pricing background reviews limited short-run predictability and longer-horizon market evidence involving valuation and changing risk compensation. That is a different object from tomorrow's peer residual or a universal pull toward an individual stock's old price. [Primary committee report, hosted by Wharton](https://rodneywhitecenter.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/NobelEconomicSciences2013_BackgroundOnAssetPricing.pdf), introduction and §§3–5.

Twenty trading sessions are about one month, not the multi-year horizon of the third question. A permanent change in earnings prospects can change the appropriate price; a past average is not automatically an equilibrium. A reversion hypothesis must say **which measured quantity reverts, toward what reference, and over what horizon**.

## What more data changes

The approved [expanded panel](../market_data/datasets/equity_daily_expanded_2016_2026-10-27/README.md) increases breadth from 30 to 200 stocks, with the same 2016–October 2026 calendar. This helps test whether peer-structure findings generalize to more names and sectors. It does not turn the existing one-day-return signal into a price-equilibrium test.

A SPY-only earlier-history probe returned no pre-2016 bars from the tested Alpaca feed/account. Much longer-horizon valuation work would need a suitable longer and differently specified dataset. More stocks do not provide more independent decades. An aggregate valuation study would require valuation/fundamental inputs that this price-bar collection does not contain.

No new trading or long-run equilibrium test is authorized or implied by recording these distinctions. Existing research output remains gross/pre-fee, and already examined history remains exploratory.
